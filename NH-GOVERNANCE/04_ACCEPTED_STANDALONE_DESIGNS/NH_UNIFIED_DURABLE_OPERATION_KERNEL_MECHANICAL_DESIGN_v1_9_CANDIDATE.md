## 1. PLAIN MEANING

Underneath N.H there are many different real jobs: reading a root, retrying a failed technical step, holding an item until there is enough to be responsible, ingesting a capture, promoting a quarantined reading, opening Personal Mode, delivering one visible answer, verifying an authority artifact, running a slow deep analysis while a fast live voice keeps talking.

Each of those jobs already has an **owner** — an accepted package that decides what the job means, when it may proceed, what counts as done, and what may never happen. Those owners are not touched here.

What they do **not** share today is the plumbing underneath them: the way a job is named so a restart can find it again; the way a job records that it got half-way; the way a job says "I am waiting, and this exact record is why"; the way a job's result is stamped with the exact world-state it was computed under, so a late result cannot quietly pretend to be current; the way a parent job that spawned three children knows which of them truly finished; the way a crash is survived by **looking things up** instead of guessing or re-doing.

This file designs that plumbing, and only that plumbing. It is deliberately stupid. It carries typed references to other people's truth. It never invents truth of its own, never decides whether something *should* happen, never counts a retry, never releases a hold, never approves an output, and never says an operation succeeded when the component that actually owns the outcome has not durably said so.

The one-sentence test this whole file is built to pass: **the kernel coordinates and references owner truth; it does not replace owner truth.**

## 3. PURPOSE AND BOUNDED SCOPE

### 3.1 What the kernel is

A **shared DUMB execution-coordination layer** that makes the following mechanics coherent across N.H components: **operation identity; waiting; cancellation and supersession coordination; retry references; duplicate prevention; checkpointing; crash recovery; stale-result rejection; terminal evidence; and the one-operation/one-log relationship.**

It is a **carrier and a fence**, in exactly two senses:

- **Carrier** — it holds typed, checkable references to other components' identities, claims, checkpoints, results, and terminal evidence, so that a restart can find them and a stale result can be recognized.
- **Fence** — it refuses to advance its own coordination state, refuses to acknowledge, and refuses to declare its parent operation complete unless the owner-declared evidence has durably committed.

Both roles are **mechanical**. Neither is evaluative.

### 3.2 What the kernel is not

The kernel is **not** a reasoning model, **not** the Meaning Engine, **not** a policy engine, **not** an autonomous planner, **not** an authority engine, **not** a retry system, **not** a hold system, **not** an ingestion system, **not** a promotion system, and **not** a privacy, access, or relevance gate.

Concretely, and bindingly:

- It never classifies an outcome as retryable — **B9 does**.
- It never decides that waiting is right, and never releases a wait — **A29 and B-HOLD do**.
- It never reserves a root ID, holds the ingestion claim, or crosses the append fence — **B11 does**.
- It never judges evidence sufficiency or authorizes production — **B16 does**.
- It never decides that a proposal is substantively valid or invalid — **B24 / §7G do**.
- It never verifies an authority artifact or mints a snapshot — **AIC does**.
- It never decides whether material may be considered or shown — **§7Q, SACL/PBR, §7P, and §7R do, in their accepted order**.
- It never decides that cancelling is the right thing to do — **the owner or a later declared policy does**.

### 3.3 Membrane placement (§0A)

The kernel sits **entirely on the DUMB side of the membrane**. It performs no interpretation, produces no meaning, forms no belief, ranks nothing, and scores nothing. It moves typed references and refuses to proceed when they do not line up. Every judgment it appears to make is in fact a **reference comparison**: does the identity that this result was bound to still equal the identity that is current? That comparison is mechanical and has exactly two outcomes — **match** or **stale** — and neither outcome is an opinion about content.

### 3.4 Scope fence for this candidate

**In scope:** the kernel-owned shared coordination mechanics enumerated in §§A–R below, plus the generic dual-model coordination support in §S that is mechanically available now.

**Out of scope, and untouched:** Addition 3, Addition 4, and Addition 5 of the five-additions decision package; any B-CYCLE completion; Master or Map integration; Register assignment; implementation of any kind; and the parked acceptance-button / acceptance-record package.

## A. COMMON OPERATION ENVELOPE — LOGICAL CONTRACT

### A.1 What the envelope is, and what it is not

The **kernel operation envelope** `[proposed]` is the **logical content contract** for what a kernel coordination operation must be able to carry so that admission, waiting, generation fencing, cancellation coordination, checkpointing, crash recovery, terminal aggregation, acknowledgement, and §0B logging can all work without any component's truth being copied, re-derived, or replaced.

Three binding statements about the envelope itself:

1. **These are LOGICAL CONTENT REQUIREMENTS, not a schema.** No final persisted field name, no serialization, no ordering, no encoding, no storage layout, and no syntax is fixed by this file. All of that **remains open** (§Open Items).
2. **The envelope carries references, not copies.** Where the envelope names an owner's identity, decision, claim, checkpoint, or evidence, the envelope holds a **typed reference to that owner's own durable record**. A referenced value is never lifted out of its owner's record and stored as a second usable truth. Where a comparison needs a value (for example, a generation identity), the envelope holds the **identity under which the comparison must be made**, and the comparison resolves against the owner's current record at use time.
3. **A reference is not an authorization and not evidence.** Carrying a privacy-owner result reference does not make the kernel a privacy gate. Carrying a claim reference does not give the kernel the claim. Carrying terminal-evidence references does not let the kernel manufacture a terminal. This is the same discipline the accepted cross-package work already states for recordkeepers and coordinators: they coordinate and record; they are never policy or authorization owners.

### A.2 The envelope elements

### A.3 Envelope rules

- **ENV-1 — Completeness at the fence where each element is due (§D).** Envelope elements are **not** all due at the same moment, and the contract's "required" marking states *that* an element is required, never *that it is required at initial UDOK-1*.
  - A **Layer-1** element — one the operation contract requires **before kernel coordination for a generation may begin** — must be resolvable at **that generation's** UDOK-1.
  - A **Layer-2** element — a component-owner prerequisite created or resolved during later child / component work (component claims, reservations, component admission, per-effect authorization, per-use privacy / access / relevance decisions) — is required **only at its owning component's exact seam, immediately before the particular effect it protects** (AF-7).
  - **Before it is due, the absence of a Layer-2 element is "not yet due", never failure** (AF-8).
  - **Once it is due, an element that is absent, unreadable, or ambiguous fails closed** at that fence.
  - The kernel **never synthesizes, acquires, derives, reserves, invents, or substitutes** a missing owner result or owner prerequisite — at either layer.
- **ENV-2 — Typed, never generic.** Every reference is typed to the exact identity kind it names (§B). A reference of one kind may never be supplied where another kind is required, even if both happen to be representable the same way.
- **ENV-3 — Append-only.** Envelope content accretes through committed coordination records. **Bindings are never edited in place, never silently rebound, and never repaired by overwrite.** A changed binding is a new generation (§H) or a new operation (§H.6), never a mutation.
- **ENV-4 — No lossy summarization.** A reference is never replaced by a paraphrase, a rollup, a score, a boolean, or a status word standing in for the owner's record. Recovery must always be able to reach the owner's actual record from the envelope.
- **ENV-5 — Field names remain open.** Nothing in this file fixes the persisted name, type, encoding, or grouping of any element above.

## B. TYPE-SAFE IDENTITY RELATIONSHIPS

### B.1 The rule that governs this whole section

**THE KERNEL `operation_id` IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY A COMPONENT DUPLICATE-PREVENTION KEY.**

The component's canonical identity **remains the sole duplicate-prevention authority at that component's seam**. The kernel **carries, checks, and references** that identity. It **never replaces, re-derives, normalizes, or overrides it** — not for convenience, not for uniformity, not during recovery, and not when the component's identity is temporarily unreadable (that case fails closed; see FC-3).

This is not a preference. It is the direct consequence of the accepted architecture: B9's *"one retry group per source-operation duplicate-prevention identity, with the source seam's own canonical key as the sole duplicate-execution defence"*; B11's bidirectional one-to-one claim↔root-ownership binding; B16's one permanent promotion claim per reading; B10's `reread_claim_key` *permanently distinct* from the new-root `enqueue_key`; B-INT-6's deliberate separation of `output_operation_id` from `delivery_idempotency_key` from `delivery_attempt_id` — a separation whose conflation was the exact defect an independent audit caught in B-INT-8 v1.1 and which v1.2 corrected by consuming B-INT-6's identities verbatim. A generic kernel identity that silently stood in for any of these would re-create precisely that defect at every seam at once.

### B.3 Non-collapse rules

- **IDR-1 — No cross-kind substitution.** No identity in §B.2 may be supplied, defaulted, inferred, or coerced into the place of another kind. A missing required identity is a **fail-closed** condition, never a fallback to a nearby identity.
- **IDR-2 — No generic reuse of numbers.** No ordinal, counter, sequence, timestamp, or version number is shared across identity kinds. In particular: a kernel generation number is never compared with, ordered against, or reused as a component generation, an AIC generation, a Personal Mode epoch, a B9 attempt number, or a delivery attempt number.
- **IDR-3 — Derivation direction is one-way.** A component identity may be **referenced** by kernel records. A kernel identity may **never** be an input to a component's canonical duplicate-prevention identity. If a component's identity were to include the kernel operation identity, the same real effect could acquire two different canonical keys under two coordination operations — which is exactly the duplicate-execution hole every accepted package exists to close.
- **IDR-4 — Attempt-scoped identities never widen.** An attempt identity (B9 attempt, delivery attempt) scopes only that attempt. Nothing attempt-scoped ever becomes an operation identity or a duplicate key, and nothing operation-scoped is ever narrowed to an attempt.
- **IDR-5 — Mutable authorization facts stay out of duplicate keys.** Following B-INT-6's accepted rule, no duplicate-prevention identity the kernel carries or checks may incorporate mutable authorization facts (mode epoch, generation, privacy decision, access level, PBR state, observability, owner version, or delivery fence). Those are **fence inputs**, checked at use time (§H), never key material.
- **IDR-6 — Type survives recovery.** Recovery never loosens typing to make a match. An identity that cannot be read as its declared kind is unreadable, not approximately matched (FC-3).

## C. COMMON LIFECYCLE SEMANTICS

### C.1 What this section defines — and what it must not become

This section defines the **semantic lifecycle facts** the kernel must be able to represent about **its own coordination operation**. Literal state names remain **proposed and open**.

**Binding limitation, stated before the states themselves:** these semantic facts **do not replace, summarize, or stand in for any accepted component state machine**. B11's ownership-generation paths and WB/LB boundaries, B16's reservation and commit-fence states, B-HOLD's `H0–H3` / `HR0–HR3` paths, B10's `RR0–RR5` + `RR-PR`, B9's `R0–R4`, B24's eight mutually-exclusive parent terminals and five permanently distinct outcomes, B-INT-5's stage-qualified mode generations and `personal_mode_delivery_fence`, B-INT-6's `delivery_unknown` — **all remain exactly as their accepted packages define them, and none is ever mapped into a lossy generic enum here.**

In particular, **`delivery_unknown` is not a kernel state**. It is B-INT-6's own unresolved, **nonterminal**, exclusive outcome. The kernel represents that situation only as *"this required terminal evidence has not durably committed; the owner's record says what it says"* — which is a **reference**, not a re-classification.

| KS-10 | Unresolved / indeterminate | The outcome genuinely is not known, and no owner has committed a determinate terminal | **Never converted to success, failure, or a retry by the kernel** |

| KS-12 | Kernel-parent terminal committed | Every terminal reference this operation contract declares required is durably committed, and the kernel parent terminal has itself committed | Only then may KS-13 exist (§L) |
| KS-13 | Acknowledgement / release eligible | The operation may be acknowledged to its caller, and coordination resources it holds (kernel coordinator claim only) may be released | Never before KS-12 (§L.4) |

### C.3 Kernel transaction boundaries

The kernel's own durable coordination transitions occur at these boundaries `[proposed labels UDOK-0 … UDOK-8, including UDOK-6C, plus the non-gating UDOK-R]`. Each is **one atomic commit with its own append-only coordination record**.

| Boundary | Commits | Single-winner? | Gating? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UDOK-0** | Kernel operation establishment: the operation identity, class, contract version, parent reference, and initial generation | Yes — one winner per establishment identity basis (§O.1) | Yes: nothing governed proceeds without it |
| **UDOK-1** | Admission fence (Layer 1): the resolved **Layer-1** prerequisite reference set for that generation, committed together with the admitted fact. **Where AIC applies, that set always includes the current, released, committed AIC authority snapshot reference (P1, AF-10); where AIC does not apply, P1 is absent — there is no third, delayed case.** It does **not** carry, grant, or stand in for any Layer-2 owner-seam prerequisite | Yes — one admitted commit per {operation, generation} | Yes: governed kernel coordination work for that generation may not proceed before it |
| **UDOK-2** | Child registration: a typed child reference with its owner, identity, generation, and the contract role from which required-for-terminal is later determined. Children may be registered **at any point while that generation remains both CURRENT and OPEN** — but always **BEFORE that child's own owner work begins under that generation** (MC-7). A generation that is **no longer current**, because a successor committed at UDOK-4, was already closed at its exact membership head as a prerequisite of that transition (UG-10), and **never receives a further registration and never receives a membership backfill** (MC-4, MC-9, R-19, FC-36). Each registration **advances that generation's child-membership head** by compare-and-append (§M.6). It is **scoped to exactly one generation** (MC-11). It is a **membership / spawn fence and nothing more**: it grants no component admission, no claim, no authorization, no retry admission, no privacy, access, or relevance result, and no right to cause any effect (MC-8) | Append-once per **{operation, generation, child owner, child identity}** (O-7, MC-11); the membership head advance is single-winner | **Yes — as a membership / spawn fence: no owner work for that child proceeds under that generation before it commits** (MC-7). It also **races UDOK-6C**, exactly one wins, and an unregistered required child cannot later be counted toward terminal. A registration under **another** generation satisfies nothing here (MC-11) |
| **UDOK-3** | Checkpoint registration: a coordination checkpoint referencing the owner checkpoints and durable facts known at that point | Append-once per checkpoint identity | No |
| **UDOK-4** | Generation transition: creation of a successor kernel generation at the **prerequisite-resolution position (KS-2)**, and the marking of the prior generation as no longer current. It carries **no** prior-generation execution state forward as already-admitted, it may **never** commit after UDOK-7, it may **never** commit while an unresolved predecessor obligation remains unaccounted for (§H.8), and it may **never** commit unless the declared predecessor generation's **UDOK-6C membership closure has durably committed and still binds that predecessor's exact, unchanged current child-membership head** — revalidated at commit time as readable, as belonging to exactly that declared predecessor, and as still current (UG-10, §M.6, FC-41) | Yes — one successor per {operation, predecessor generation} | Yes for anything binding to the new generation — and the successor becomes runnable only after **its own** UDOK-1. **The predecessor's committed exact-head closure is a prerequisite of this boundary**, so no successor ever exists over an unclosed predecessor membership set (UG-10, UG-11) |
| **UDOK-5** | Cancellation or supersession **request** commit | Append-once per request identity | No — a request never stops anything by itself (§G) |
| **UDOK-6** | Terminal-evidence registration: recording that a specific required owner terminal has durably committed, by reference | Append-once per {operation, generation, required terminal reference} | Yes for the terminal contract |
| **UDOK-6C** | **Generation membership closure**: commits that this generation is **closed to further child registration**, binding the exact **child-membership head** it closed at (§M.6) | Yes — one closure per {operation, generation}, by compare-and-append against the current membership head | Yes: **UDOK-7 may not commit without it, and neither may a UDOK-4 that supersedes this generation** (UG-10, FC-41). It remains **membership closure only** — not permission, not terminal evidence, not acknowledgement, not proof that cancellation stopped, and not authority to transition (MC-3) |
| **UDOK-7** | Kernel-parent terminal commit, gated by the **current generation's** required-terminal set as determined from durable contract and owner truth **against a verified, unchanged, closed membership head** (§M.5, §M.6). Where this generation is a **successor**, its predecessor's own exact-head closure was already a prerequisite of the UDOK-4 that created it (UG-10), so every generation behind it in the chain is closed at a proven head | Yes — one terminal per kernel operation, ever | Yes |
| **UDOK-8** | Acknowledgement / release-eligibility commit | Yes — one per operation | Yes |
| **UDOK-R** | Recovery coordination record: append-only record of what recovery found and what it appended | Append-once per recovery action identity | **Non-gating** — recovery records never substitute for owner truth |

**Boundary rules.**
- **TB-1** — No two boundaries share a transaction. Each commits alone, atomically, with its own record.
- **TB-2** — Every gating boundary is a **compare-and-commit against durable state**, never against in-memory belief.
- **TB-3** — **UDOK-7 may never commit before every required UDOK-6 has committed.** **UDOK-8 may never commit before UDOK-7.** These two orderings are the kernel's structural defence against generic success (§R.10).
- **TB-4** — No boundary ever writes into, edits, or completes a component record. The kernel writes only kernel coordination records.

### C.6 Compare-and-commit requirements

Every gating boundary commits **only if** the durable precondition it names is still true at commit time. Specifically:

- **UDOK-0** commits only if no committed establishment already exists for the same establishment identity basis. A concurrent second attempt **loses**, is recognized as a duplicate, and is recorded as duplicate prevention against the existing operation.
- **UDOK-1** commits only if the operation's current generation at commit time equals the generation the **Layer-1** prerequisite set was resolved under, and every **Layer-1** reference still resolves — **including, where AIC applies, the current, released, committed AIC authority snapshot reference, which must resolve at AIC before this commit and before any governed work under that generation** (AF-10). It never waits on, and never pre-resolves, a Layer-2 owner-seam prerequisite.
- **UDOK-2** commits only if the generation it declares is the operation's **current** generation at commit time **and** that generation is **still open** — no UDOK-6C closure has committed for it — and only by **compare-and-append against that generation's current child-membership head** (§M.6). **Both conditions are revalidated at commit time and neither is ever waived**: a generation superseded by a committed UDOK-4 was already closed at its exact head as a prerequisite of that transition (UG-10), so it is **never registered into and never backfilled** (MC-9, R-19, FC-36). Its append-once identity is **generation-scoped**: `{operation identity, generation, child owner, child identity}` (O-7, MC-11). A registration committed under **any other generation** never satisfies this one, never advances this head, and never permits owner work here (MC-7, FC-37); a **same-generation** duplicate resolves only against that generation's existing registration. A loser — whether it lost to a concurrent registration or to closure — is **refused and durably recorded**, never dropped (MC-1), and that child does **no owner work under that generation** (FC-36, FC-37).
- **UDOK-4** commits only if the declared predecessor generation is still the current generation, **no kernel-parent terminal (UDOK-7) has committed** for this operation, **every unresolved predecessor obligation has been accounted for under §H.8** (resolved by recovery, or covered by the owning component's own durable safe-supersession evidence), and **that predecessor generation's UDOK-6C membership closure has durably committed** — which the kernel **revalidates at commit time** as (i) **readable**, (ii) **bound to exactly that declared predecessor generation of exactly this operation**, and (iii) **still binding that predecessor's exact, unchanged current child-membership head** (UG-10, §M.6, MC-4). A missing, unreadable, wrongly-bound, or head-advanced closure means **UDOK-4 is refused, recorded honestly, and recovery-first** (FC-41). The closure is its **own separate atomic boundary** against the predecessor's own head (TB-1, O-11): it is never folded into, implied by, or performed as part of the transition — a still-open predecessor is closed at **UDOK-6C first**, and only then may a successor be attempted. Committing that closure remains **membership closure only** and confers **no authority to transition** (MC-3); it is one prerequisite among the §H.8 prerequisites, all of which still apply in full. This is a single compare-and-append, so concurrent or stale writers can neither fork the generation chain nor skip a generation, and a terminated operation can never acquire another generation. The loser is refused and recorded, and may re-derive against the new current generation. The successor is created at the prerequisite-resolution position and becomes runnable only after its own UDOK-1. This is the same head discipline the accepted B20 alias chain uses, applied here to kernel generations only.
- **UDOK-6** commits only if the referenced owner terminal record is durably readable **at its owner** at commit time. The kernel never registers a terminal from a message, a return value, a callback, or an in-memory belief.
- **UDOK-6C** commits only if the declared membership head is still that generation's current head — a single compare-and-append against the same head that UDOK-2 advances, so **child registration and closure have exactly one winner** (§M.6). The loser is refused and durably recorded, never dropped. The head it binds is the **one** membership head afterwards read by that generation's terminal-completeness determination (MT-7) **and** by any UDOK-4 that supersedes that generation (UG-10) — closure, completeness, and transition never read a different head from one another. Closure remains **membership closure only**: not permission, not terminal evidence, not acknowledgement, not proof that cancellation stopped, and not authority to transition (MC-3).
- **UDOK-7** commits only if **all** of the following hold: this generation's **UDOK-6C closure has committed**; the **membership head recorded by that closure is still the current head** and is **identical to the head the terminal-completeness determination bound to**; the **current generation's required-terminal set was determined from durable contract and owner truth** against that exact head (§M.5, §M.6); every member of that set has a committed UDOK-6; no cancellation/supersession request has left the operation in an unresolved stop state (KS-9); no unresolved predecessor obligation remains unaccounted for (§H.8); **no unreconciled membership contradiction stands against this generation** — that is, no durable owner work by a child that never registered **under it** has been found against it, or, where such a contradiction was recorded, the owning component's or the applicable policy owner's **typed durable resolution reference has been validated and consumed** (MC-10, MC-11, MC-12, FC-38, FC-39); where this generation is a **successor**, the UDOK-4 that created it already revalidated its predecessor's exact-head closure, so no unclosed predecessor membership set stands behind this terminal (UG-10); and no kernel-parent terminal already exists. The determination is part of the terminal-completeness fence: the set is fixed **at** that fence, against a closed and verified membership state, and may not be widened or rewritten behind it, nor narrowed after a failure.
- **UDOK-8** commits only if UDOK-7 has committed **and no unreconciled membership contradiction stands against the generation UDOK-7 committed against**. A contradiction discovered **after UDOK-7 but before UDOK-8** refuses the acknowledgement while it stands unreconciled, and does so **without erasing, rewriting, recomputing, or re-deciding the committed terminal**, which is preserved as history beside the contradiction and its prior-terminal integrity-violation evidence (MC-10 (B), FC-38). A contradiction discovered **after UDOK-8 never unwinds it**: the acknowledgement stands as history, the contradiction and its prior-terminal and prior-acknowledgement integrity-violation evidence are appended beside it, **exactly one acknowledgement still ever exists**, and none is re-issued (MC-10 (C), O-9, §L.7, FC-40). At **both** of those positions — (B) and (C) — **no successor generation of this same logical operation may exist or be created**, whatever any resolution reference names: the operation's generations ended at UDOK-7, and onward treatment may only be a genuinely new logical operation or another authority-valid correction that reopens, regenerates, rewrites, revokes, and reissues nothing (§H.7, MC-10 (B)/(C), MC-12, FC-27, FC-39).

### C.7 Transition evidence

Every semantic transition is carried by **exactly one append-only kernel coordination record**, which states: the operation identity; the generation; the boundary; the durable precondition that was compared; the typed references that changed; and, where the transition depends on an owner, **the reference to the owner's own record that made it true**.

- A transition record is a **canonical state record**, **not** a §0B operational log (§N.3).
- A transition record **never** contains a copied owner value standing in for the owner's record.
- A transition record is **never** rewritten. A correction is a new record, appended, referencing what it corrects.

Here too **no successor generation of this same logical operation may follow**: onward treatment may only be a genuinely new logical operation, or another authority-valid correction that does not reopen, regenerate, rewrite, revoke, or reissue either record (§H.7, §L.6, O-9, MC-10 (C), MC-12, FC-27, FC-39) |

### C.9 What can and cannot be acknowledged

- **Can be acknowledged:** an operation at KS-12, whose every contract-required terminal reference is registered at UDOK-6 against durably committed owner records.
- **Cannot be acknowledged:** KS-6 (waiting), KS-7/KS-8 with unknown completion, KS-9, KS-10, partial terminal sets, a superseded generation, any operation whose required terminal evidence is unreadable, or any operation against which an **unreconciled closed-generation membership contradiction** stands (MC-10, MC-12, FC-38). Each of those is reported **as what it is** — waiting with its owner and reason, unresolved, recovery-required, or indeterminate — and **never as success and never as a silent failure**.
- **Already acknowledged, and never unwound:** where UDOK-8 has committed, a contradiction discovered afterwards is **recorded beside it** and never converts it into a non-acknowledgement, a revocation, a re-issue, or a claim that it was prevented (§L.7, MC-10 (C), O-9, FC-40).

## D. ADMISSION / PREREQUISITE FENCE

### D.1 Two fence layers, deliberately separated

There are **two distinct fence layers**, and this file keeps them apart because collapsing them would either demand that component truth exist before its owner creates it, or let a kernel record stand in for an owner's fence. Neither is permitted.

**Layer 1 — the kernel admission fence (UDOK-1).** The single kernel boundary at which an operation proves that **every prerequisite the operation contract genuinely requires BEFORE kernel coordination for that generation may begin** has been resolved against its own owner's durable record. That, and only that, is what UDOK-1 governs.

**Layer 2 — the owner-seam fences.** Component-specific prerequisites that are **created or resolved during later child / component work** — component claims, reservations, component admission, per-effect authorization, and per-use privacy / access / relevance checks — **stay at the owning component's exact accepted seam**. They are not kernel admission prerequisites, they do not exist yet at initial UDOK-1, and **the kernel never requires them to**.

**The pre-effect rule that connects the two layers:** the kernel **may require, check, and record a reference to a Layer-2 prerequisite immediately BEFORE the particular component effect that prerequisite protects** — and **must not require all of them to exist at initial UDOK-1**. The kernel still never acquires, derives, or invents any of them; the owner creates them, at the owner's own seam, under the owner's own rules.

Both layers are **evidence resolution, not evaluation**. The kernel asks only: *does the required reference exist at the fence where its owner requires it, is it readable, is it of the declared type, and does it still resolve at commit time?* It never asks whether the referenced decision was correct, sufficient, current enough, or wise. Those questions belong to the owners.

### D.2 The prerequisite classes

| Class | Fence layer — where it is required | Required when | Resolved against | Kernel's role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Authority basis** | **Layer 1 (UDOK-1), always, wherever AIC applies** — proved at **that generation's** UDOK-1 **before any governed work** under that generation, and repeated at each successor generation to which AIC still applies. Where AIC does **not** apply to the operation, **P1 is absent**. **There is no third, delayed case** (AF-10) | The operation contract declares authority verification applicable — and then it is a **pre-operation** requirement, not a mid-flight one | A **current, released, committed AIC authority snapshot** (ID-8) | Carry and check the reference; never verify, never mint, never re-verify |
| **P2 — Policy reference** | **Layer 1** where the contract requires the adopted policy record to exist before coordination begins; otherwise **Layer 2**, at the owner's seam | The owner contract requires an adopted policy record to exist for the step | The owning policy package's record | Carry the reference; never select or interpret policy |

### D.3 Fence rules

- **AF-1 — Resolution before governed work, scoped to Layer 1.** No governed kernel coordination work for a generation proceeds before **that generation's own** UDOK-1 commits. UDOK-1 governs **only** the Layer-1 prerequisites the operation contract requires before coordination for that generation may begin; it is **not** the fence for Layer-2 owner-seam prerequisites.
- **AF-2 — Missing, unreadable, or ambiguous evidence fails closed at the fence where it is due.** At **Layer 1**, the operation moves to KS-9 (recovery required) or stays at KS-2, records the failure honestly, and proceeds with nothing. At **Layer 2**, the single coordination step immediately preceding the protected effect is refused and recorded honestly, and that effect does not proceed. **Evidence that is not yet due is not missing evidence** (AF-8); the operation simply has not reached that fence.
- **AF-3 — The kernel never synthesizes a missing owner result.** Not a default, not an assumed pass, not an inherited result from a sibling operation, not a cached result from a previous generation, not a value reconstructed from a summary.
- **AF-4 — Order is the owner's, not the kernel's.** Where accepted packages fix an order — **§7Q before §7R** on every internal path; **§7Q first and SACL/PBR second** at the visible-output door, both gates approving the same immutable payload — the kernel resolves references in that order and **never re-orders, merges, or short-circuits it**.
- **AF-5 — Re-fencing on generation change.** A successor kernel generation (§H) requires its **own** UDOK-1, and enters at the prerequisite-resolution position (KS-2), never at an already-admitted position. Prerequisites resolved under a superseded generation **never** carry forward — not automatically and not by request.
- **AF-6 — Per-use re-resolution where the owner requires it.** Where an owner's accepted contract requires a decision to be resolved **before every use** — B-INT-8's §12C current-use resolution, B-INT-5's and B-INT-6's revalidation of mode, epoch, generation, owner version, observability, and the delivery fence immediately before delivery — the admission fence does **not** substitute for that per-use check. The kernel carries the reference and enforces nothing beyond the check the owner performs at its own door.
- **AF-7 — The pre-effect reference fence (Layer 2).** Before a kernel coordination step that immediately precedes a **particular component effect**, the kernel must be able to resolve the Layer-2 prerequisites protecting **that** effect — the component's own claim and canonical duplicate-prevention identity, the authorization its seam requires, and the privacy / access / relevance results its fence prescribes. If any of them is absent or unreadable **at that point**, the coordination step fails closed (FC-2, FC-3). This fence protects one effect at one moment; it is never generalised backward into UDOK-1 and never forward into a blanket permission.
- **AF-8 — No pre-emptive requirement, and no substitution.** The kernel **never** requires a component prerequisite to exist earlier than its owner creates it, **never** treats its absence at UDOK-1 as a failure, and **never** acquires, derives, reserves, or invents one to satisfy its own fence. A component prerequisite that does not exist yet is simply not yet due — the kernel proceeds only as far as the owner's seam allows and waits there under §I, typed to that owner.
- **AF-9 — Owner fences are never weakened.** Nothing in the Layer-1 / Layer-2 separation removes, relaxes, reorders, or pre-approves any component fence. Every accepted seam — B9 admission, B11's ingestion claim and append commit fence, B16's reservation and promotion commit fence, B10's claim, B-HOLD's release-evidence interface, B24's validation boundary, B-INT-4's authorization phases, B-INT-5's delivery fence, B-INT-6's two-gate output chain, B-INT-8's route commits — performs its own check at its own boundary, in full, exactly as its accepted package states it.
- **AF-10 — AIC is pre-operation, in exactly two cases, with no third.** Where the operation contract declares authority verification applicable, the **current, released, committed AIC authority snapshot (ID-8) is a mandatory Layer-1 prerequisite at that generation's UDOK-1**, and **no governed work under that generation proceeds before it is resolved there**. Each **successor** generation repeats that proof at **its own** UDOK-1 wherever AIC still applies (AF-5) — a predecessor's snapshot reference never carries forward as current. Where AIC does **not** apply to the operation, **P1 is simply absent**, and its absence is neither a failure nor a deferral. **There is no third case**: the authority basis is never postponed to a later owner-prescribed point, never resolved mid-flight, and never resolved after governed work has begun. A snapshot reference that is **missing, unreadable, unproved, or not released fails closed at UDOK-1** (FC-1) — nothing is admitted, nothing proceeds, and the honest reason is recorded. A **changed or unprovable authority input routes only through AIC's own mechanics** (§D.4); the kernel never repairs, refreshes, extends, re-derives, or works around it. This rule fixes **when the governing basis must be proved**, and nothing else: the snapshot remains **governing-basis proof only** (§D.4) — never permission, never privacy authorization, never retry admission, never an operation identity, never success evidence — and **no TTL is invented here**. It also moves **nothing** out of Layer 2: component claims and canonical duplicate-prevention identities (P7), per-effect authorization (P3 by default), and the per-use privacy, access, and relevance results (P4, P5, P6) **remain Layer-2 owner-seam prerequisites**, exactly as stated above.

### D.4 What an AIC snapshot is — and is not

The AIC authority snapshot is **proof of governing basis**: it says that a specific authority condition was proven, content-bound, under AIC's own accepted mechanics.

**When it is due — two cases, never three (AF-10).** Where AIC applies, the **current, released, committed** snapshot is a **mandatory Layer-1 prerequisite at that generation's UDOK-1, before any governed work under that generation**, and each successor generation to which AIC still applies proves it again at **its own** UDOK-1. Where AIC does not apply to the operation, **P1 is absent**. Nothing here creates a delayed or mid-flight authority check: a governing basis proved **after** governed work began would not be a governing basis at all. If the snapshot is **missing, unreadable, unproved, or unreleased at that fence, the operation fails closed there** (FC-1) — it is not admitted, it proceeds with nothing, and the unproven condition is named honestly.

**What that timing does not do.** Being Layer 1 makes the snapshot **earlier**; it does not make it **more**. It remains governing-basis proof and nothing else, it authorizes no effect, and it moves no Layer-2 owner-seam prerequisite forward with it (AF-10). Component claims, per-effect authorization, and the per-use privacy / access / relevance results stay exactly where their owners put them.

It is **NOT**:

- an operation identity;
- a permission;
- a privacy authorization;
- retry admission;
- success evidence.

Further binding statements:

- **No snapshot TTL is invented here.** Any time-based validity window, expiry, refresh, or scheduled/background re-verification **remains open with AIC** (§Open Items). The kernel neither assumes a snapshot stays valid nor assumes it expires.
- **Changed or unprovable AIC inputs route ONLY through AIC's own mechanics** — its current-input re-proof, its fresh establishment episode, and its successor-generation behaviour, which produces a successor generation **only after a fully revalidated seal**. There is no kernel-side alternative route, no partial route, and no route that lets governed work continue meanwhile. The kernel never repairs, refreshes, extends, or re-derives a snapshot, and never treats an unproven condition as provisionally proven.
- **AIC's two outward outcomes remain exactly two** — a proven snapshot, or a stop that names the unproven condition. The kernel adds no third.
- **AIC's own outcome routing into accepted B9 classes stays AIC's**, including the accepted constraint that no AIC deterministic refusal uses the B24-oriented one-careful-substantive-retry path. The kernel neither re-routes nor supplements that mapping.
- **How a caller learns of a later AIC outcome remains open** (§Open Items). This file designs the reference the caller would carry, not the notification mechanism.

### D.5 Kernel admission is not component admission

UDOK-1 admits **kernel coordination work only**. It is not B9 retry admission (§J), not B11 ingestion admission, not B16 promotion reservation, not B10 claim admission, not TSC authorization, and not delivery dispatch.

**Component admission and component claim acquisition happen later, at the component's own seam, during that component's own work** — after UDOK-1, typically inside a registered child operation. That component performs its admission **at its own boundary, under its own rules**, acquires its own claim, and commits its own fence. The kernel records that it resolved, references it before the effect it protects (AF-7), and does nothing else.

**Consequently:** an operation that has passed UDOK-1 has proved its Layer-1 prerequisites and nothing more. It has **not** been granted any component admission, holds **no** component claim, and carries **no** component authorization. Those come into existence only when their owners create them.

## E. CHECKPOINT CONTRACT

### E.1 The rule first

**The kernel references owner checkpoints. It does not design a generic replacement for component checkpoints.**

B11's per-generation append commit fence and its recovery rows, B16's reservation phases, B10's `RR` boundaries, B-INT-4's TSC checkpoints and continuation linkage, B-INT-5's crash seams, B-INT-6's dispatch-intent flush — each of those is that component's own durable position-keeping, and each stays exactly where it is. What the kernel adds is a **coordination checkpoint** that makes those owner checkpoints **findable and correctly interpreted after a restart**, across a multi-owner operation.

### E.2 What a kernel coordination checkpoint must make recoverable

A committed kernel checkpoint (UDOK-3) must be sufficient for recovery to establish, **without inference**:

| # | Recovery-sufficiency requirement | Carried as |
|---|---|---|
| CP-1 | **Which logical kernel operation** this checkpoint belongs to | The kernel operation identity (ID-1) |
| CP-2 | **Which kernel generation** it was taken under | The kernel generation (ID-2) |
| CP-3 | **Which owner/component operation(s)** were in flight | Typed references to each component operation identity |
| CP-4 | **Which canonical identity** governs each component effect involved | Typed references to the component-owned canonical duplicate-prevention identities (ID-4) — referenced, never re-derived |
| CP-5 | **Which prerequisite snapshot / version references** were in force | Two groups, kept distinct: the **Layer-1** references resolved at **this generation's** UDOK-1 (**the released, committed AIC snapshot reference wherever AIC applies to this operation — recorded as resolved at that generation's UDOK-1, never as pending and never as deferred (AF-10); absent entirely where AIC does not apply**, plus any policy or authorization reference the contract requires before coordination begins); and the **Layer-2** owner-seam references resolved later, each recorded against the component effect it protects (component claims and canonical identities, per-effect authorization, and the per-use privacy / access / relevance results at their owner-prescribed fences). A Layer-2 reference that had not yet been created at checkpoint time is recorded as **not yet due**, never as missing |
| CP-6 | **Which claims and effects are already durable** | Typed references to owner claim records and committed-effect records |
| CP-7 | **Which child operations have terminal evidence** | Per-child references to registered terminal evidence (UDOK-6), distinguishing required from non-required children |
| CP-8 | **What remains incomplete** | For the **current generation**: the required terminals not yet registered, as classified by the contract's already-fixed rules (§M.5), and the registered children whose owners' records were not yet terminal. Where one exists, also the **unreconciled membership contradiction** standing against that generation, its **discovery position** — (A) before UDOK-7, (B) after UDOK-7 before UDOK-8, or (C) after UDOK-8 — the fact that only the owner's or policy owner's typed durable resolution reference can reconcile it, and **which dispositions are mechanically legal at that recorded position** — a same-operation successor generation at **(A) only**, through the valid UDOK-4 path, and at **(B)** and **(C)** never (MC-10, MC-12, §H.7). Where this generation is a **successor**, the checkpoint additionally carries the **predecessor's closure reference and the exact head it closed at**, as the transition prerequisite that UDOK-4 revalidated — never as permission, and never as a substitute for this generation's own closure (UG-10, CPR-4) |
| CP-9 | **What recovery owner must be consulted next** | The named owning component and its **accepted recovery interface** for each incomplete item |

### E.3 Checkpoint rules

- **CPR-1 — A checkpoint is a reference bundle, not a snapshot of anyone's data.** It never copies owner state, never summarizes it, and never becomes a source that could disagree with the owner.
- **CPR-2 — Append-once per checkpoint identity.** Duplicate registration of the same checkpoint identity is recognized, skipped, and recorded as duplicate prevention (§O.4).
- **CPR-3 — Generation-bound.** A checkpoint belongs to exactly one kernel generation and is never carried forward into a successor generation as if it were current. A successor generation takes its own checkpoints.
- **CPR-4 — Never authorizing.** A checkpoint permits nothing. It does not extend a claim, refresh a prerequisite, keep a snapshot alive, or hold anything open.
- **CPR-5 — Contradiction fails closed.** If two committed checkpoints for the same {operation, generation} disagree, or if a checkpoint references an owner record that does not resolve, recovery reads **the owner's record as the truth**, and where that cannot be resolved, the operation is **recovery-required** (FC-6).
- **CPR-6 — Checkpoints never make a partial operation look complete.** A checkpoint recording that two of three required children are terminal is exactly that fact, and never an aggregate "mostly done" that could be acknowledged (§P).
- **CPR-7 — Checkpoint frequency, granularity, storage, and retention remain open.** Nothing here fixes when a checkpoint is taken beyond the requirement that recovery be able to establish CP-1 … CP-9 from durable state.

## F. LOOKUP-FIRST CRASH RECOVERY

### F.1 The twelve recovery rules, and the thirteenth stated separately

Recovery of a kernel coordination operation proceeds by **looking up durable truth in a fixed order**, and never by re-doing work to find out what happened.

1. **Establish the kernel operation identity.** Recovery begins from the committed establishment record, never from a reconstructed or re-derived identity.
2. **Establish the current kernel generation.** The generation chain head is read first; an ambiguous head is fail-closed (FC-4), never resolved by picking one.
3. **Read committed kernel coordinator state.** Admission, checkpoints, child registrations, request records, registered terminals — all read from durable records only, never from memory, never from a caller's assertion.
4. **Read AIC and current prerequisite references.** Each required reference is resolved **at its own owner**. A reference that no longer resolves is a fail-closed condition, not a reason to proceed on the old value. **Where AIC applies, the released, committed snapshot is read first, as the Layer-1 pre-operation basis for the current generation (AF-10)** — recovery never resumes governed work under a generation whose authority basis is unproven, unreadable, or unreleased, and never postpones that proof to a later point.
5. **Locate owner claims before acquiring new ones.** Recovery never asks a component for a fresh claim until it has looked for the claim that may already exist — this is the accepted lookup-first discipline B9, B10, B11, B16, and B-HOLD each state for themselves, applied to kernel coordination.
6. **Locate committed component effects before repeating work.** If the effect is durably committed at its owner, the work is done; the kernel registers what it finds and repeats nothing.
7. **Locate child terminal evidence.** Each registered child's terminal is looked up at that child's owner.
8. **Invoke the owning component's accepted recovery interface** for each incomplete item. The kernel never performs a component's recovery on its behalf and never substitutes its own reasoning for the component's recovery rules.
9. **Reject stale-generation work.** Any result, effect proposal, or acknowledgement bound to a superseded generation fails the current-generation fence (§H) and is preserved as history.
10. **Append only genuinely missing coordination records.** Recovery writes a coordination record only where durable truth exists and the corresponding kernel record is absent. Every recovery action is itself one recorded operation (UDOK-R).
11. **Never infer success from absence.** The absence of a failure record, the absence of a worker, the absence of an attempt event, the absence of a channel response, and the absence of a correction are each **absence** — never confirmation. This is the same discipline B-INT-6 fixes for delivery (*after durable dispatch intent, the absence of a later attempt event never proves that no call happened*), B-INT-7 fixes for capture (*the absence of a BOP observation does not prove the microphone never began*), and OOP fixes for outcomes (*absence of correction is never confirmation*).
12. **Never reconstruct owner truth from a summary**, a log, a cached value, a rollup, a report, or a kernel coordination record. Kernel records point at owner truth; they never stand in for it.

**Rule 13, stated separately because it is the most frequently violated in ordinary systems:** **never repeat an external or stored effect merely because an acknowledgement was lost.** A lost acknowledgement is recovered **by lookup** at the effect's owner. Where the owner's record shows the effect committed, the acknowledgement is completed from that record. Where the owner's record is genuinely unknown — B-INT-6's `delivery_unknown` being the canonical case — the state stays **unresolved and nonterminal**, and **no blind resend, no automatic duplicate attempt, and no replay occurs**.

### F.3 Recovery record rules

- Every recovery action is **itself one recorded operation** (UDOK-R), append-only, referencing what it read and what it appended.
- Recovery records are **non-gating**: they never authorize, never substitute for owner truth, and never become evidence for the subject of the operation.
- Recovery **never edits** a prior coordination record; it appends.
- Recovery may run repeatedly; it is idempotent by identity (§O.10).

## G. CANCELLATION AND SUPERSESSION COORDINATION

### G.1 Mechanics only

The kernel designs **generic mechanics** for expressing, recording, propagating, and recovering cancellation and supersession. It designs **no policy whatsoever** about when either is appropriate.

**The kernel MUST NOT choose when cancellation is substantively appropriate.** The owner, or a later separately declared policy, decides that. In the live dual-model case specifically, *whether* obsolete heavy work should be cancelled, superseded, supplemented, continued, or restarted **remains open** (§S.4, §Open Items).

### G.2 What a request may carry

A cancellation or supersession **request** record (UDOK-5) may carry:

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Request kind | Cancellation or supersession — typed, never interchangeable |
| Requesting owner | Which owner or authorized caller made the request |
| Scope | The exact scope the request applies to — one operation, a declared child set, or a scoped set bound to an owner-defined scope reference (for example B-INT-5's `mode_authority_dependency_ref`, which exists precisely so that separately authorized background work is **not** swept up by a scoped cancellation) |
| Target operation / generation | The exact operation identity and generation the request targets |
| Cause / evidence reference | A reference to the durable record that occasioned the request |
| Owner acknowledgement reference | Where the owning seam has durably acknowledged receipt |
| Owner terminal evidence reference | Where the owning seam has durably committed a terminal reflecting the stop |

### G.3 The invariants

- **G-INV-1 — Cancellation-requested is not cancellation-complete.**
- **G-INV-2 — Supersession-requested is not supersession-complete.**
- **G-INV-3 — An operation is not recorded stopped until the owning seam provides durable evidence.** Not on request. Not on timeout. Not on the disappearance of a worker. Not on silence.
- **G-INV-4 — Unknown cancellation state remains unresolved / recovery-required.** It is never rounded to "cancelled", never rounded to "still running", and never rounded to "failed".
- **G-INV-5 — Obsolete results fail the current-generation fence even if the old worker may still be running.** The fence is about **binding**, not about liveness. A result bound to a superseded generation cannot commit or be delivered regardless of whether anything is still executing.
- **G-INV-6 — Committed past effects remain history.** A supersession does not undo, delete, retract, or rewrite anything already durably committed. N.H's no-destruction law and the accepted preservation discipline apply.
- **G-INV-7 — Supersession controls future use and future commits** — unless an **owning component** has a separately authorized corrective mechanism of its own (B-INT-8's §12C current-use resolution being the accepted example, where later corrections control current use while history stays immutable). The kernel never invents such a mechanism and never performs a correction on an owner's behalf.
- **G-INV-8 — Scope is honoured exactly as declared.** A request never widens beyond its declared scope, never cascades into unrelated operations, and never sweeps up separately authorized work.
- **G-INV-9 — No physical stop mechanics are designed here.** No process kill, no thread interruption, no signal, no timeout value, no cancellation latency, and no scheduling behaviour is invented (§Open Items).

### G.4 Propagation

Propagation is **reference propagation**, not force. The kernel may:

- record the request against the target operation and generation;
- register the request reference on registered children **within the declared scope**, so each child's owner can see it through its own records;
- record each owner's acknowledgement when the owner durably acknowledges;
- register the owner's terminal evidence when it durably commits (UDOK-6).

The kernel may **not**: stop a child; mark a child cancelled; discard a child's claim; withdraw a child's authorization; or treat an unacknowledged request as effective.

### G.5 Interaction with retry, holds, and terminals

- A cancellation request is **not** a failure classification and **never** enters B9 as one. B9 classifies outcomes; the kernel does not hand it a verdict.
- A cancellation request never releases, satisfies, or bypasses a live B-HOLD hold.
- **Terminal stays terminal**: a request never reopens an operation whose owner has durably committed a terminal, and never converts a substantive rejection into something re-runnable.
- A cancellation request never becomes a reason to retry, and never becomes a reason to re-deliver.

### G.6 Crash behaviour

Covered by R-20, R-21, and R-22. Summarised: the **request** survives; the **stop** does not exist until the owner's durable evidence exists; unknown completion is **recovery-required**, and every result bound to a superseded generation is stale (§H).

## H. GENERATION AND STALE-RESULT FENCING

### H.1 The generic contract

The kernel maintains, for each coordination operation, a **current kernel generation** (ID-2) advanced only by UDOK-4 under compare-and-append against the current head (§C.6).

**Every result consumed by the current operation must be bound, at the moment it was produced, to the typed references under which it was produced.** Depending on the seam, that binding set may include:

- the kernel operation identity;
- the kernel generation;
- the component operation identity;
- the component canonical input identity;
- the AIC snapshot / generation reference, where authority verification applies;
- the privacy decision reference and the access generation, where applicable;
- any other owner version or fence that seam requires — for example B-INT-5's mode runtime epoch and stage-qualified mode generations, its owner-version and observability state, and the `personal_mode_delivery_fence`; B-INT-6's revalidation set immediately before delivery; B-INT-8's §12C current-use resolution.

### H.2 The fence, and where it applies

**Before** any of the following, the current owner truth must still match the result's bound references:

1. **consuming a result**;
2. **committing an effect**;
3. **releasing a result**;
4. **delivering visible output.**

Where the owning component performs its own version of this check at its own door, **the component's check governs at that door**; the kernel's fence never replaces it, never pre-approves it, and never lets a result skip it.

### H.3 Mismatch = stale

A mismatch on **any** bound reference makes the result **stale**. There is no partial match, no tolerance, no "close enough", and no automatic re-binding.

### H.4 What happens to stale work

Stale work:

- **is preserved as history** — recorded honestly, never deleted, never destroyed;
- **cannot commit into the current generation**;
- **cannot be delivered**;
- **cannot become authority**;
- **cannot silently update its bindings** — no rebinding, no refresh, no "adopt the new generation";
- **does not become a retry merely because it is stale.** Staleness is not an outcome classification. Whether anything further should happen is the owner's question, and where retry is involved at all, **B9 alone** classifies and admits it (§J).

### H.5 Stale / invalidation evidence

When a mismatch is detected, the kernel records a **stale/invalidation evidence record** (E26): the operation, the generation compared, the reference that mismatched, the result's binding, and the current owner truth's identity. That record is a canonical state record — **not** a §0B operational log, **not** a failure verdict, and **not** a trigger for anything.

### H.6 Changed inputs and successor behaviour — without inventing policy

- **Changed kernel-coordination circumstances** that leave the operation's identity intact **and occur before the kernel parent terminal** advance the **kernel generation** (UDOK-4) — and only where the predecessor generation's **UDOK-6C closure has durably committed at that predecessor's exact unchanged current membership head**, revalidated at that commit (UG-10, FC-41). The successor is created at the prerequisite-resolution position (KS-2), requires **its own** UDOK-1 (AF-5), and takes fresh bindings. No prior-generation admission, checkpoint, binding, or execution state carries forward as current.
- **Changed component canonical inputs** do **not** advance a kernel generation. They create **a new source-operation identity and a new B9 retry group according to the accepted owner rules** — B9's own canonical-input identity rule. The kernel neither performs nor second-guesses that; it carries the new references.
- **Changed or unprovable AIC inputs** route through **AIC's own** re-proof and successor-generation mechanics (§D.4).
- **Whether a change should produce continuation, supplementation, cancellation, or restart is policy, and stays open** (§S.4, §Open Items).

### H.7 Terminal finality and generations

**Terminal stays terminal.** The generation mechanism is a pre-terminal mechanism only.

- **A kernel operation whose parent terminal (UDOK-7) has committed may NOT acquire another generation.** UDOK-4 is refused after UDOK-7, always (FC-27).
- **A successor generation exists only over a predecessor whose membership is already closed at its exact head.** UDOK-4 requires the declared predecessor generation's **committed UDOK-6C**, revalidated at that commit as readable, as belonging to exactly that predecessor, and as still binding its **unchanged current membership head** (UG-10, §C.6, FC-41). The pre-terminal generation mechanism therefore never leaves a **superseded-but-unclosed** predecessor behind it, and never produces a successor whose transition is unbound to a complete, stable predecessor membership set.
- **Acknowledgement / release eligibility (UDOK-8) may NOT be followed by a generation change.** Once acknowledged, the operation is finished; it is never restarted inside its own identity.
- **Prior-generation checkpoints and bindings remain history and are never current for a successor.** They stay readable, preserved, and referenceable as history; they are never treated as this generation's admission, this generation's checkpoint, or this generation's binding. A result bound to a prior generation is **stale** under §H.3 regardless of how nearly complete it was.
- **Later genuinely new work after a terminal requires a new logical operation**, established under its owner's normal identity rules, with its own establishment identity basis (O-1), its own contract, its own generations, and its own terminal. It does **not** reopen, extend, revive, or re-generation the completed operation. **This holds however the later work is routed, and whoever routes it.** No owner disposition, no policy disposition, and no typed durable resolution reference may produce a successor generation of an operation whose UDOK-7 has committed — whether the acknowledgement has followed (**position C**) or not (**position B**). The only onward treatments that mechanically exist there are a **genuinely new logical operation** or **another authority-valid correction that does not reopen, regenerate, rewrite, revoke, reissue, or add a second terminal or acknowledgement** (§L.7, MC-10 (B)/(C), MC-12, FC-27, FC-39). A same-operation successor generation exists at **position A only**, and only through the valid UDOK-4 path with every §H.8 prerequisite.
- **This changes no owner's rules.** Whether later work is genuinely new — and whether it should continue, supplement, cancel, or restart anything — is **policy and stays open** with its existing owners (§S.4, §X).

### H.8 Unresolved predecessor obligations — a successor generation is never an escape hatch

**The principle.** A kernel generation change is **not** a B9 retry, and **not** a way past anything an owner has not resolved. **KS-9 (recovery required) and KS-10 (indeterminate) are recovery-first**, and a successor generation is not an exit from either. This preserves, in kernel coordination terms, the accepted principle that `indeterminate_recovery_required` means **recovery first — no retry and no admission until the owning seam resolves it**. The kernel does not restate or re-own that rule; it refuses to route around it.

**What counts as an unresolved predecessor obligation.** Anything under the predecessor generation that **could still have produced an effect, or could conflict with later work**, and whose outcome the owner has not durably resolved. At minimum: an owner claim whose disposition is unknown after a crash; a component effect that may or may not have committed; a required terminal that is unreadable or absent; an unknown delivery outcome (B-INT-6's `delivery_unknown`, which is unresolved and **nonterminal**); a cancellation or supersession whose completion is unknown; and any other owner truth the owning seam has left unresolved.

**The rules.**

- **UG-1 — Recovery-first by default.** From KS-9 or KS-10, the kernel's first and only default move is **recovery** (§F): establish identity, establish the generation, read committed coordinator state, and consult **the owning component's accepted recovery interface**.
- **UG-2 — UDOK-4 may not discard an obligation.** A generation transition may not commit while an unresolved predecessor obligation remains unaccounted for. Refused, recorded honestly, recovery-first (FC-33).
- **UG-3 — Two, and only two, ways past.** Either (a) **recovery resolved it** — the owning component's durable record now says what happened, and the kernel registers what it found; or (b) the **owning component's own durable safe-supersession evidence** proves that the particular outstanding computation is **effect-free and safely supersedable**, and the kernel consumes **that owner's evidence by reference**. These two exhaust the ways past an **unresolved obligation** only; the predecessor generation's committed exact-head membership closure is a **separate, additional** UDOK-4 prerequisite that neither of them satisfies (UG-10).
- **UG-4 — The kernel never classifies safe supersession itself.** It never decides that an old computation was effect-free, never infers it from the kind of work, never assumes it because nothing was seen, and never derives it from elapsed time or a missing worker. **If no owner proof exists: fail closed and recovery-first** (FC-33).
- **UG-5 — Never infer occurrence or non-occurrence.** The kernel never concludes from a generation change, from silence, from a vanished worker, or from an absent record that an old effect did or did not occur (§F.1 rule 11).
- **UG-6 — Indeterminate is never converted into stale-and-forgotten.** Staleness (§H.3) fences a result's **binding**; it does not resolve an outcome and it does not discharge an obligation. An indeterminate predecessor outcome stays **indeterminate**, recorded and open, even where its result is also stale (FC-16, FC-35).
- **UG-7 — No owner's gate is routed around.** A successor generation never moves past a live **B-HOLD** hold, a **B9** state, a **privacy** refusal, an **unknown delivery** outcome, or any other owner's recovery state. Each remains exactly what its owner says it is (FC-34).
- **UG-8 — Committed history is untouched.** Resolving an obligation never rolls back, replays, deletes, or rewrites anything already durably committed (§G-INV-6, PC-2).
- **UG-9 — No policy is decided.** Whether the situation should ultimately produce continuation, supplementation, cancellation, or restart is **policy and stays open** with its existing owners (§S.4, §X). §H.8 governs only what the kernel may do **mechanically** before it may safely move past an obligation.
- **UG-10 — A successor generation requires the predecessor's committed membership closure, at that predecessor's exact unchanged head.** UDOK-4 may not commit unless the declared predecessor generation's **UDOK-6C has durably committed**, and the kernel **revalidates at commit time** that the closure is **readable**, that it **belongs to exactly that declared predecessor generation of exactly this operation**, and that it **still binds that predecessor's exact, unchanged current child-membership head** (§M.6, MC-4, §C.6). The reason is mechanical, not stylistic: UDOK-2 requires a **current, open** generation, and R-19 backfill requires a generation that is **still current and still open** — so a successor committed over an unclosed predecessor would strand that predecessor **superseded but never closed**, no longer current for registration and never closed at a proven head, leaving it **no coherent executable membership-recovery path** and leaving the successor's transition unbound to a complete, stable predecessor membership set. Closure is its **own separate atomic boundary** against the predecessor's own head (TB-1, O-11): it is **never folded into, implied by, or performed as part of** the transition. A still-open predecessor is closed at **UDOK-6C first**, and only then may a successor be attempted. Missing, unreadable, wrongly-bound, or head-advanced closure ⇒ **UDOK-4 refused, recorded honestly, recovery-first** (FC-41). **Closure remains membership closure only** — committing it is not permission, not terminal evidence, not acknowledgement, not proof that cancellation stopped, and not authority to transition (MC-3). It is **one** prerequisite alongside UG-2 and UG-3, which continue to apply in full.
- **UG-11 — An already-committed successor without a valid predecessor exact-head closure is a durable contradiction that fails closed.** Where recovery finds a committed successor generation whose declared predecessor has **no committed UDOK-6C**, or one that is unreadable, bound to another generation, or no longer binding that predecessor's unchanged final head, that is a **transition / membership-closure contradiction** — not a repairable bookkeeping gap. It is **recovery-required and fail-closed**: every record — successor, predecessor, membership state, closure record, and owner records alike — is **preserved exactly as it stands**; **owner truth is looked up first**; **no closure and no membership is fabricated, backdated, or inferred**; **no effect is replayed**; **no registration is backfilled into the superseded predecessor** (MC-9, R-19, FC-36); and **no governed successor work, no UDOK-7, and no UDOK-8** may proceed while it stands unresolved. Its onward resolution runs on the same terms as a membership contradiction: the **owning component's or the applicable policy owner's** own typed durable resolution, which the kernel **validates and consumes and never chooses**, and which may itself only name a disposition that is mechanically legal at the position it is taken from (MC-12, R-15c, FC-42). **The kernel decides no owner or policy disposition here either.**

## I. WAITING / HOLD REPRESENTATION

### I.1 What the kernel may carry

For an operation that is blocked (KS-6), the kernel may carry:

- **wait owner** — which component owns this block;
- **wait reason** — typed to that owner's own vocabulary, not to a generic one;
- **owner record reference** — the exact durable record at the owner that constitutes the block;
- **release interface / reference** — where the owner has one, the reference by which release becomes visible.

That is the whole of it. The kernel represents the block; it never *is* the block, and it never ends one.

### I.2 The typed wait kinds must not be collapsed

**Do not collapse them into one generic retryable waiting state.** Each kind is different in what it means, who ends it, and what may happen while it lasts.

| Wait kind | Owner | Ends when | Kernel's role | Never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B-HOLD hold** | B-HOLD (mechanics) with A29 (the meaning of "enough") | B-HOLD commits release on **verified bound release evidence** | Carry B-HOLD's **exact hold identity** and, on release, B-HOLD's **exact release evidence reference** | **Never create another hold record**; never release; never queue around; never retry around; while held, every consuming class stays excluded fail-closed, and silent use is a logged violation |
| **Technical retry delay** | B9 | B9's own admission, gaps, deadlines, and budgets decide | Reference B9's retry group and attempt | Never schedule, never count, never wait on the kernel's own clock (§J) |
| **Privacy refusal** | §7Q (privacy owner) | Only the privacy owner's own record changes it | Carry the refusal reference | Never treat a privacy refusal as retryable, temporary, or waitable; never retry around it; never seek a second opinion elsewhere |
| **Access limitation** | SACL / PBR | The access owner's own record | Carry the reference and the access generation | Never widen; never re-ask a different gate |
| **Dependency block** | The named dependency owner (e.g. B16's promotion-side view of a B-HOLD fact) | That owner's record | Carry the typed reference | Never re-classify as technical |
| **Indeterminate / unresolved** | The owner whose outcome is genuinely unknown | Recovery-first: the owner's record, when it resolves | Carry the reference, remain recovery-required | Never convert into success, failure, retry, or a hold |
| **Component-specific wait** | That component | That component's rules | Carry it **typed to that component** | Never generalise it into a shared wait type |

### I.3 Additional binding statements

- **W-1** — The three shared boundaries of the accepted Bundle-1 coordination note bind here unchanged: **retry ≠ reread; held blocks both; terminal stays terminal.**
- **W-2** — **Holding never forces a missing reading or interpretation into being**, and a hold may sit on an item or reading that already exists; while held, it gains no new ordinary downstream use and cannot silently influence memory, ranking, interpretation, snapshots, or output. The kernel's waiting representation carries that fact and adds nothing to it.
- **W-3** — **A29 remains the owner of what "enough" means**, including which insufficiency holds rather than producing an honest `insufficient_context` reading. The kernel never applies, approximates, or shortcuts that test.
- **W-4** — **No wait duration, checking frequency, expiry, or unsatisfiable-condition detection is designed here.** All of that remains open with B-HOLD/A29 (§Open Items).
- **W-5** — Waiting is **never a verdict**. A waiting operation has not failed, has not succeeded, and is not indeterminate; it is waiting, with a named owner and a referenced reason.

## J. B9 RELATIONSHIP

### J.1 What B9 exclusively owns

**B9 exclusively owns:** outcome classification; retry eligibility; `R0`–`R4`; the retry group; attempt identity; admission; the one-live-attempt rule; attempt counting; budgets; gaps; deadlines; exhaustion; real-change continuation; and retry disposition.

The **accepted B9 retry values are not open and are not reopened here.** They are named for completeness of reference only, exactly as their accepted package and closure record state them: **3 total technical attempts per episode**; minimum gaps of **10 seconds then 30 seconds** on live paths and **1 minute then 3 minutes** on background/nightly paths; real elapsed deadlines of **7 minutes live** and **15 minutes background/nightly**, with the attempt gate and the elapsed gate both applying and **whichever closes first stopping retry**; **exactly one careful substantive retry** after a durable rejection record; permanent monotonic attempt numbering coexisting with per-episode ordinals 1–3, including the later-episode ordinal-1 admission with its explicit no-gap and deadline-pending durable states; the non-exhaustive **real-change** rule with its append-only real-change record and separate one-winner consumption record; early stop where retry is useless or unsafe; and truthful exhaustion with unfinished state preserved.

**The kernel does not restate those values as its own, does not apply them, and does not choose among them.**

### J.2 What the kernel may do

The kernel may **carry and coordinate B9 references**: the retry-group reference (E16), the attempt reference (E17), and the durable records B9 itself produces. It may record that an operation is waiting on a technical retry delay owned by B9 (§I.2). It may fence a result produced under one attempt against the current generation (§H).

### J.3 What the kernel must not do

The kernel must **not**:

- **admit retries** — admission is B9's `R0`-boundary single-winner decision, requiring an existing declared budget or a recorded policy-plus-authorization reference, fail-closed otherwise;
- **count attempts independently** — no kernel-side counter, ordinal, or tally exists;
- **schedule its own retry** — no kernel timer, gap, backoff, or deadline exists;
- **define alternate retry classes** — B9's classes are the classes;
- **open another retry group** — one group per source-operation duplicate-prevention identity, established by B9;
- **retry around a hold, a privacy refusal, or an indeterminate state** — each of those refuses admission at B9 and stays refused.

### J.4 Changed inputs

**Changed source canonical inputs create the new source-operation identity and a new B9 group according to the accepted owner rules.** The kernel carries the new references and never derives them.

### J.5 Retry is not reread, and neither is the other

A retry re-attempts the **same** canonical identity to complete its one outcome and can never add a layer. A reread is a **new** identity per recorded trigger and can never complete or replace an earlier operation's outcome. **The kernel never converts one into the other, and never lets a coordination record blur them.**

### K.3 The kernel coordinator claim

Where a single-winner mechanism is genuinely needed for the kernel's **own** state transition, the kernel may hold a **kernel coordinator claim** (ID-3). Its scope is exhaustively:

- **it protects kernel coordination truth only** — that exactly one writer commits a given kernel coordination transition;
- **it grants no component effect authority whatsoever**;
- it never extends, substitutes for, refreshes, or stands in for any owner claim;
- it never survives as an implied authorization after release;
- holding it is not permission to do anything except commit the kernel coordination record it protects.

### K.4 Effects

- A **committed effect is history**. Supersession, cancellation, recovery, and generation change do not undo it (§G-INV-6, §P).
- The kernel may hold a **reference** to a committed effect (E22) so recovery can find it without repeating it. That reference is not a right to act on it.

## L. TERMINAL EVIDENCE AND ACKNOWLEDGEMENT

### L.1 The four distinct things

| Thing | Owner | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner / component terminal evidence** | The owning component | That component's own durable record that its operation reached a terminal, in that component's own accepted terminal vocabulary |
| **Required-child terminal references** | The kernel operation contract freezes the deterministic **rules, roles, and criteria** that determine which children are required — **not** a concrete future child list (§M.4); the children's owners produce the terminals themselves | Which child terminals this parent's completion actually depends on, resolved for the **current generation** at the terminal-completeness fence against a closed, verified membership head (§M.5, §M.6) |
| **Kernel parent terminal evidence** | Kernel | The kernel coordination operation's own single terminal (UDOK-7) |
| **Acknowledgement / release eligibility** | Kernel | That the operation may be acknowledged to its caller and its kernel coordination resources released (UDOK-8) |

### L.2 The completion rule

**The kernel may mark itself successfully complete only when all terminal evidence that the operation contract's already-fixed rules make required for the current generation — determined at the terminal-completeness fence, against a closed and verified child-membership head (§M.5, §M.6) — has durably committed.** Every required terminal is registered individually at UDOK-6, per {operation, generation, required terminal reference}, against the owner's own durable record, looked up at the owner.

**And the membership the determination runs against must itself be honest.** Because every child registers **before** it works (MC-7), a closed generation's membership is the complete set of children that were permitted to work under it — and **only registrations made under that generation count toward it** (MC-11). Where recovery nonetheless finds durable owner work by a child that never registered **under it**, the parent may not be marked complete around it: a **still-current, still-open** generation reconciles membership before closure (MC-9), and a **closed** one — or one no longer current, because a successor committed over its proven closed head (UG-10) — yields a **recovery-required contradiction** that keeps an **uncommitted** UDOK-7 and UDOK-8 refused until the owning component's or the applicable policy owner's **typed durable resolution reference** reconciles it (MC-10, MC-12, FC-38). Where the terminal — or the terminal and the acknowledgement — had **already** committed when the contradiction was discovered, they **stand as history** with the contradiction recorded beside them (§L.7).

### L.3 Generic technical signals are never sufficient

**A generic technical "process exited 0" or "worker returned" is never sufficient where the owner requires stronger terminal evidence.** Neither is a return value, a callback, an in-memory flag, a message on a queue, the absence of an error, or the disappearance of a worker.

Where an owner's accepted contract names the evidence, that evidence is the evidence. Examples carried by reference, not restated as new rules: B-INT-4's **verified durable `bai_token_consumed` receipt** as the authorization commit point and the sole durable post-crash proof that token consumption succeeded — where neither the durable claim nor B15's committed transaction may substitute for it; B-INT-6's requirement that delivery outcome be the channel's truth to tell, with `delivery_unknown` remaining unresolved and nonterminal; B16's single `production_eligibility_record` per claim; B24's eight mutually-exclusive parent terminals; B11's exactly-one terminal parent operational record per ingestion operation.

### L.4 No acknowledgement before the required terminal

**No acknowledgement occurs before the required terminal.** Not on partial completion, not on a superseded generation, not on an unknown stop, not on an indeterminate outcome, and not because a caller is waiting.

An operation that cannot be acknowledged is reported **as what it is**: waiting with its owner and reason; unresolved; recovery-required; indeterminate; or partially complete with the exact remaining required terminals named (§P). **No fake success and no hidden failure.**

### L.5 Lost acknowledgement

**A lost acknowledgement after a durable terminal must recover by lookup, not by effect replay.** Recovery reads the owner's terminal record and the kernel's own terminal record and completes the acknowledgement from them (R-26). Nothing is re-executed, re-sent, or re-committed to produce an acknowledgement.

### L.6 One terminal, once

Exactly **one** kernel parent terminal may ever exist for one kernel operation. A second attempt is recognized as a duplicate, refused, and recorded as duplicate prevention — never written as a second terminal truth.

### L.7 A membership contradiction discovered after the terminal, or after the acknowledgement

**Neither a committed terminal nor a committed acknowledgement is ever unwound by a later discovery.** Where recovery finds durable owner work by a child that never registered under a **closed** generation (MC-10, MC-11), and the parent had already committed:

- **its terminal (UDOK-7) but not its acknowledgement (UDOK-8)** — the terminal **stands as history**, the membership contradiction and explicit **prior-terminal integrity-violation evidence** are appended beside it, and the **acknowledgement is refused** while the contradiction stands unreconciled (MC-10 (B), R-19b, FC-38);
- **its acknowledgement (UDOK-8)** — the terminal **and** the acknowledgement **both stand as history**, and the membership contradiction plus explicit **prior-terminal and prior-acknowledgement integrity-violation evidence** are appended beside them (MC-10 (C), R-19c, FC-38).

**What is never said about the prior acknowledgement.** It is **never** described as prevented, erased, revoked, rewritten, re-issued, or as not having happened. It happened, it is recorded, and the contradiction is recorded next to it as exactly what it is (FC-40). The same holds for the prior terminal.

**What still holds unchanged.** §L.6's **one kernel parent terminal, ever**, and **O-9's one acknowledgement, never re-issued**, are untouched: a contradiction refuses a **new** terminal or a **new** acknowledgement and never produces a second one, and it never re-opens a terminated operation (§H.7, FC-27). While it stands unreconciled the contradiction is **recovery-required**, and the kernel places **no further reliance on, makes no reuse of, performs no republication of, takes no release action on, and issues no new acknowledgement of** the contradicted completion claim (FC-38, FC-39).

**How it is reconciled, and what stays open.** Only by the **owning component's or the applicable policy owner's typed durable resolution reference**, which the kernel **validates and consumes** and **never chooses** (MC-12, R-19d, §T I-13). **Which substantive route to take is theirs, and it stays open** (MC-2, §S.4, §X) — but the routes that mechanically exist **here** are fewer than at position A, because the terminal has already committed. At **both** of these positions a **successor generation of this same logical operation is not among them** (§H.7, FC-27). The onward treatment may only be a **genuinely new logical operation**, established under its owner's normal identity rules with its own establishment identity basis (O-1), its own contract, its own generations, and its own terminal; or **another authority-valid correction** that does **not** reopen, regenerate, or rewrite the completed operation, does not issue a second terminal (§L.6), and — at position (C) — does not revoke, rewrite, or reissue the acknowledgement (O-9). A typed durable resolution reference naming a disposition that is **not** mechanically legal for its recorded position **reconciles nothing**, is refused, and leaves the contradiction standing (MC-12, FC-39).

## M. PARENT / CHILD OPERATIONS

### M.1 Parenting without merging

A kernel coordination operation may **parent** component operations. Parenting is a **reference relationship**, never a merge.

**Each child keeps its own:** identity; owner; canonical duplicate-prevention key; state machine; claims; checkpoints; terminal evidence; and **§0B log**.

**Membership comes first.** Parenting is a reference relationship, but it is not a retrospective one: a child **belongs to** a kernel parent's generation only once its **UDOK-2 registration has durably committed under that generation** (MC-11), and **that commit precedes the child's own owner work under that generation** (MC-7). Membership is the only thing that commit establishes — the child's admission, claim, authorization, and every privacy, access, and relevance decision still happen afterwards, at that child's own owner seams, in full (MC-8).

### M.2 What the kernel parent does

- **References child records** by typed identity (E4, UDOK-2).
- **Aggregates declared completion** — that is, it registers which **required** child terminals have durably committed, and computes nothing beyond that registration.
- **Does not duplicate child state as a second source of truth.** No mirrored status field, no rollup that could disagree with the child, no cached child outcome, no parent-side re-classification of a child's terminal.

### M.3 What the kernel parent never does

- Never completes, cancels, releases, retries, or repairs a child.
- Never inherits a child's claim, budget, checkpoint, authorization, or terminal.
- Never lends a child its own authority — a child's effect is authorized only by that child's own owner fence (§K.1).
- Never writes into a child's log (§N).
- Never lets a child begin owner work, or cause an effect, under one of its generations before that child's **UDOK-2 has durably committed** (MC-7), and never treats a registration appended after the fact as though the fence had been kept (MC-9).
- Never lets its own registration record substitute for anything the child's owner must still do — admission, claim, authorization, retry admission, or any privacy, access, or relevance decision (MC-8).
- Never counts an unregistered child toward its terminal contract without first registering what durably exists **and only where that generation is still both current and open** (R-19); where the generation is already closed, or is no longer current because a successor committed at UDOK-4 over its proven closed head (UG-10), it never registers into it and instead records the contradiction honestly, at whichever of the three durable discovery positions it lands (R-19a … R-19c, MC-10).
- Never treats a registration committed under **one** generation as membership in **another** (MC-11).

### M.4 Required versus non-required children — rules are frozen, concrete children are not

The operation-contract version (E2) freezes the **deterministic rules, roles, and criteria** that determine which child operations and terminal slots are required. It does **not** require every future concrete child identity to be known when the whole kernel operation is first established.

- A non-required child's outcome is recorded but never gates the parent terminal; a required child's terminal always does.
- **Concrete children may be registered later** (UDOK-2), at any point during a generation, according to those already-fixed rules — always **before that child's own owner work begins under that generation** (MC-7). "Later" means later than the operation's establishment; it never means later than the child's own work. Each registration is **scoped to the generation it commits under** (MC-11).
- **New information may lead owner mechanics to spawn new children** — under the current generation, or under a successor generation where one is mechanically available (**pre-terminal only**, through the valid UDOK-4 path over a predecessor already closed at its exact head — UG-10, §H.7) — **without rewriting the original operation contract.** The rules do not change; the population they classify does.
- **No policy is invented here** about *when* new information genuinely requires a new child, a continuation, a supplement, a cancellation, or a restart. Those choices remain open with their existing owners (§S.4, §X).

### M.5 Determining the current generation's required-terminal set

**The terminal-completeness fence.** Before UDOK-7 may commit, the kernel must be able to determine, **from durable contract and owner truth**, the **complete required-terminal set for the CURRENT generation** — that is, apply the contract version's already-fixed rules to the durably registered children and terminal slots of that generation, and resolve which of them the rules classify as required.

The rules that make this safe:

- **MT-1 — Determination precedes the terminal.** The set is determined at the terminal-completeness fence, from durable records only — never from memory, never from a caller's assertion, never from a summary.
- **MT-2 — Never narrowed after failure.** The required set may **never** be narrowed, reclassified, or trimmed after something failed, stalled, or went unreadable, merely to make the parent look complete. That is fake success (§P, PC-6).
- **MT-3 — Never widened behind the check, and structurally unable to be.** Once the current generation's terminal-completeness fence has established the required set, that set must **not** be widened or rewritten behind that check — and §M.6's membership closure makes that structural rather than merely forbidden: the set is bound to an exact membership head, the generation is closed at that head, and UDOK-7 re-verifies both. New work that genuinely belongs to the operation arrives **before** the closure, or it belongs to a successor generation, or it belongs to a new logical operation (§H.7) — never retroactively behind a terminal check that has already run.
- **MT-4 — Per generation, under the same rules.** Each successor generation establishes **its own** current-generation required-terminal set, applying the **same** contract rules to that generation's own registered children — and **a registration made under any other generation is not a member here** (MC-11). A prior generation's set is history and is never the successor's set.
- **MT-5 — Undeterminable means fail closed.** If the set cannot be determined from durable contract and owner truth, UDOK-7 is refused and the operation is recovery-required (FC-29). The kernel never guesses the rules, never assumes an empty set, and never substitutes the children that happen to be registered for the set the rules require.
- **MT-6 — Registration is not classification.** Registering a child records what exists; it never decides that the child is required. The contract's rules decide that, deterministically, from the child's recorded role, at the terminal-completeness fence.
- **MT-7 — Determination binds to an exact membership state.** The determination is valid only **against the exact child-membership head it read** (§M.6). If that head has advanced, the determination is void and must be re-made against the new head. A determination is never carried across a membership change.

### M.6 Generation membership, closure, and the one-winner race rule

**The defect this closes.** Without an atomic fence, this sequence is possible: the required-terminal set is determined; a concurrent required child registers; UDOK-7 commits against the older set; the parent falsely looks complete. That is fake success arriving through a race rather than through a decision, and it is structurally prevented here.

**The child-membership head `[proposed]`.** Each generation of a kernel operation carries **one durable, append-only child-membership state** with a **single current head**. Every UDOK-2 child registration for that generation advances the head by **compare-and-append against the head it declares**. Concurrent registrations therefore serialize: each either advances the head or loses, is refused, and is durably recorded, and may re-attempt against the new head.

**The closure boundary (UDOK-6C).** Closure is a **single-winner compare-and-append against the same head**. Committing it records: the operation, the generation, and the **exact membership head at which the generation was closed**. After closure, that generation is **closed to further child registration**.

**The one safe winner, in both directions.**

| Race | Winner | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A required child's UDOK-2 commits **before** UDOK-6C | **The child** | The membership head advances. Any determination made against the older head is **void** (MT-7) and must be re-made. The child is therefore **visible to the terminal-completeness determination**, UDOK-7 cannot commit without it, and — only now — **that child's owner work may begin under this generation**, through its own owner seams (MC-7, MC-8) |
| UDOK-6C commits **before** that child's UDOK-2 | **Closure** | The generation is closed at the recorded head. The later registration attempt is **refused and durably recorded** against that closed generation — with its identity, owner, contract role, and the reason — and routed onward (below). Because registration was refused, **that child may not do owner work under that generation either**: there is no membership to work under (MC-7, MC-10) |

**One head, and every reader of it reads the same one.** The head that a generation's UDOK-2 registrations advance is the **same** head its UDOK-6C closure binds, the **same** head its terminal-completeness determination is valid against (MT-7), and the **same** head that any UDOK-4 superseding that generation must revalidate as **unchanged** (UG-10). So the race above produces **one** consistent membership head used by the predecessor's closure **and** by the transition: **child-first** advances the head **before** closure and before any transition, and that child is visible to completion accounting and may then do its owner work; **closure-first** refuses the later registration and therefore leaves that child **no owner work to do under that closed predecessor at all** (MC-7, FC-36, FC-37). There is exactly one winner, exactly one head, and no reader of that head that can disagree with another.

**MC-1 — No child ever silently disappears from completion accounting.** A registration refused because the generation was already closed is **never dropped, never discarded, and never invisible**. It stands as a durable record naming the child, its owner, its role, the closed generation, and the refusal reason, and it remains readable in completion accounting as *registered against a closed generation*. **The same holds where that generation is also no longer current**, because a successor committed at UDOK-4 over its proven closed head (UG-10): the refusal is durably recorded in exactly the same way, and the superseded generation is **never re-opened, never registered into, and never backfilled** (MC-9, R-19, FC-36).

**MC-2 — Work that legitimately belongs after a closed generation goes somewhere honest, along a route that mechanically exists where it is taken.** According to the **owner's and the applicable policy's** rules, it proceeds under a **successor generation of the same logical operation**, under a **genuinely new logical operation**, or under another **authority-valid correction**. **The kernel does not decide which** — that choice is policy and stays open (§S.4, §X) — but it does check that the chosen route mechanically exists at the position it is taken from. **A successor generation of the same logical operation exists pre-terminal only**, and only through the **valid UDOK-4 path**: every §H.8 prerequisite, plus the predecessor generation's own committed **exact-head** closure, revalidated at that commit (UG-10, §C.6, FC-41). **Once the kernel-parent terminal has committed — with or without an acknowledgement — it does not exist at all** (§H.7, FC-27), and the honest routes there are a **genuinely new logical operation** or a correction that reopens, regenerates, rewrites, revokes, and reissues nothing (§L.7, MC-10 (B)/(C)). The kernel records the refused registration and the routing reference the owner supplies; it invents neither, and it **refuses a reference naming a route the position does not have** (MC-12, FC-39).

**MC-3 — Closure is not a terminal and grants nothing.** UDOK-6C closes membership for one generation. It does not complete the operation, does not acknowledge anything, does not release anything, does not mark any child complete, and does not imply that the required terminals have committed. It is only the fence that makes the required set determinable and stable.

**MC-4 — Closure is per generation.** A successor generation opens with its **own** membership state, its own head, and its own later closure. A prior generation's closure is history and never closes a successor. **Membership itself is per generation in the same way, and so is the append-once identity of every registration in it** (MC-11, O-7). **And a successor opens only over a predecessor that is already closed:** the predecessor's own UDOK-6C, bound to that predecessor's exact unchanged head, is a **prerequisite of the UDOK-4** that creates the successor, revalidated at that commit (UG-10, §C.6, FC-41). No generation is therefore ever left **superseded-but-unclosed**, and each generation's head is closed by its own closure and by nothing else.

**MC-5 — Unreadable membership fails closed.** If the membership head, the closure record, or the binding between the determination and the head cannot be read, UDOK-7 is refused and the operation is recovery-required (FC-31, FC-32).

**MC-6 — Design level only.** Nothing here chooses the storage, representation, encoding, index, or final field names of the membership head, the closure record, the registration-before-work ordering evidence, the contradiction record, the prior-terminal / prior-acknowledgement integrity-violation evidence, or the typed durable resolution reference (MC-12). Those **remain open**.

**MC-7 — Membership is committed BEFORE owner work.** Every child of a kernel parent, under a kernel generation, must have its **UDOK-2 registration durably committed before that child's owner work begins under that generation**. Not concurrently, not immediately after, not at the child's first checkpoint, and not at the parent's terminal-completeness fence. The reason is exactly the one §M.6 exists for: work that no membership record can see is work the completeness determination cannot count, and a parent can be closed around it while it is still running. **The order is: register, then work.** Its converse — work or effect first, register afterwards — is **never legitimate**, no matter how the registration is later back-filled (MC-9).

**MC-8 — UDOK-2 is a membership / spawn fence and NOTHING else.** A committed child registration establishes exactly one thing: that this child is a member of this generation and is visible to completion accounting. It **grants no component admission, no component claim, no authorization, no retry admission, no privacy result, no access result, no relevance result, and no right to cause any effect**. Every one of those remains its owner's, at its owner's own seam, **after** registration and immediately before the effect it protects (§D.1 Layer 2, AF-7, AF-8, AF-9, §K.1). A registered child that has not yet passed its owner's seams may do nothing there; registration moved it inside the membership fence, not past anyone's gate. **Nor does registration classify the child** as required or non-required — the contract's fixed rules do that, at the terminal-completeness fence (MT-6).

**MC-9 — Historical reconciliation where the generation is still CURRENT and OPEN.** Where recovery finds that a child's owner ran without a committed UDOK-2, and that generation is **still the operation's current generation** and its **UDOK-6C has not committed**: recovery **looks up the owner's durable truth**, **reconciles membership before closure** — registering what durably exists (UDOK-2) and its terminal (UDOK-6) where the owner's records support it — **preserves that truth exactly as the owner committed it**, **replays nothing**, and **records the membership-fence violation honestly** as its own durable record (R-19). Reconciliation makes the completion accounting true; it does not make the violation retrospectively permitted, and it never becomes a sanctioned route for working first and registering later (MC-7). **Both conditions are required, and neither is ever waived.** A generation that has been **closed**, or that is **no longer current** because a successor committed at UDOK-4 over its proven closed head (UG-10), is **never** reconciled this way: **there is no membership backfill into a superseded predecessor** (FC-36). That discovery is MC-10's, at whichever of its three positions it lands.

**MC-10 — Historical reconciliation where the generation is already CLOSED, or no longer current, at whichever position the discovery lands.** Where the same discovery is made **after** that generation's UDOK-6C has committed — including where that generation has since been superseded by a UDOK-4 committed over its proven closed head (UG-10) — the kernel **never registers into it and never backfills membership into it** (MC-4, MC-9, MC-11, FC-36). The situation is an **explicit contradiction** — durable owner work exists that the closed membership state does not contain — and it is recorded as exactly that. Recovery **looks up the owner's claim, its committed effect, and its terminal**, and preserves each exactly as it stands. What differs between cases is only **what had already committed at the parent** when the contradiction became durably discoverable, and there are exactly **three** such positions:

- **(A) Discovery before UDOK-7.** No kernel parent terminal exists. The terminal **and** the acknowledgement **fail closed** while the contradiction stands unreconciled: UDOK-7 may not commit, and UDOK-8 cannot follow (R-19a, §C.6, FC-38). **This is the only position at which a successor generation of the same logical operation is mechanically possible**, and then **only** through the **valid UDOK-4 path** — every §H.8 prerequisite, plus the predecessor generation's own committed **exact-head** closure, revalidated at that commit (UG-10, FC-41). The other mechanically legal dispositions here are a **genuinely new logical operation** or another **authority-valid correction**. The owner or the applicable policy owner chooses among them; the kernel does not (MC-2, MC-12).
- **(B) Discovery after UDOK-7 but before UDOK-8.** The kernel parent terminal **already committed and is preserved as history** — never erased, never rewritten, never quietly recomputed, never re-decided. The kernel **appends** the membership contradiction record **and explicit prior-terminal integrity-violation evidence**, stating that that terminal committed against a membership state which the discovered owner work contradicts. The **acknowledgement is refused** while the contradiction stands unreconciled (R-19b, §L.7, FC-38). §H.7 and §L.6 are untouched: the terminal stays terminal, and no second terminal is ever written. **No successor generation of this same logical operation may exist here** (§H.7, FC-27) — the terminal remains history, and the only mechanically legal onward treatments are a **genuinely new logical operation** or another **authority-valid correction that does not reopen, regenerate, rewrite, or issue a second terminal**. The owner or the applicable policy owner chooses between them; the kernel only validates that what they supply is legal for **this** position (MC-2, MC-12, FC-39).
- **(C) Discovery after UDOK-8.** The kernel parent terminal **and the acknowledgement** both already committed, and **both are preserved as history**. The kernel **appends** the membership contradiction record **plus explicit prior-terminal AND prior-acknowledgement integrity-violation evidence**. It **never claims the prior acknowledgement was prevented, erased, revoked, rewritten, re-issued, or made not to have happened** — it happened, it is recorded, and the contradiction stands **durably alongside** it (R-19c, §L.7, FC-40). **O-9 is untouched**: exactly one acknowledgement ever exists for one kernel operation, and none is re-issued. **No successor generation of this same logical operation may exist here either** (§H.7, FC-27) — terminal and acknowledgement both remain history, and the only mechanically legal onward treatments are a **genuinely new logical operation** or another **authority-valid correction that does not reopen, regenerate, rewrite, revoke, or reissue either record**. The owner or the applicable policy owner chooses between them; the kernel only validates that what they supply is legal for **this** position (MC-2, MC-12, FC-39).

In **every** position: **nothing pretends the work was absent**, and nothing pretends the terminal or the acknowledgement never happened. The contradiction is **recovery-required** until it is reconciled under **MC-12**, and while it stands the kernel makes **no further reliance on, reuse of, republication of, release action on, or new acknowledgement of** the contradicted completion claim (FC-38, FC-39). Where the work legitimately belongs somewhere, it belongs — per the **owner's and the applicable policy's** rules — to a **successor generation of this same logical operation at position (A) only**, through the valid UDOK-4 path (UG-10); or, at **any** position, to a **genuinely new logical operation** or another **authority-valid correction**. **The kernel decides none of them** (MC-2, §S.4, §X); it only refuses one that is not mechanically legal for the recorded position (MC-12, FC-39).

**MC-11 — Registration is generation-scoped, and so is its append-once identity.** A child registration belongs to **exactly one** kernel generation. Its append-once identity is **`{parent operation identity, generation, child owner, child identity}`** (O-7), because membership itself is per generation (MC-4). Three mechanical consequences follow, and no fourth:

- **A registration committed under any other generation** — predecessor, successor, or unrelated — **never satisfies current-generation membership, never permits current-generation owner work (MC-7, FC-37), and never advances the current generation's membership head** (§M.6). It is not a member here, it is not visible to this generation's terminal-completeness determination (MT-4), and it may never be offered in place of a registration under this generation.
- **A same-generation duplicate** — the same child owner and child identity registered again **under the same generation** — is recognized, skipped, and recorded as duplicate prevention **against that generation's existing registration only** (O-7, DP-2). A registration under a different generation is **neither a duplicate of it nor a substitute for it**.
- **Nothing here decides anything about the child's own canonical identity.** Whether an owning component may use the same child canonical identity again under another generation, and what that would mean at that component's own seam, is **that component's question**, under its own accepted identity and duplicate-prevention rules (§B.1, ID-4, IDR-3). The kernel **neither permits nor prohibits it**, derives nothing from it, and its generation-scoped registration record makes **no statement** about it. The registration is the **kernel's** per-generation membership fact; the child's canonical identity remains wholly its owner's.

**MC-12 — Reconciliation is a typed durable owner/policy resolution reference, validated and consumed — never a kernel decision.** *"Until reconciled"* (MC-10, R-19a … R-19c, FC-38, §L.7) names an exact mechanical condition, not an indefinite wait:

- **Who supplies it.** The **owning component** of the contradicted work, or the **applicable policy owner** where the accepted contract puts the disposition there. **This creates no new owner and no new policy system** (§R.11, §T I-13): it is the same owner, or the same policy owner, that already owns that work and its disposition.
- **What it must bind, by reference.** The **original kernel operation identity**; the **generation**; that generation's **UDOK-6C closure and the exact membership head it closed at**; the **unregistered child's identity and its owner**; the **discovered owner truth** — the claim, the committed effect, and the terminal, as they durably stand; the **contradiction record** itself; and, **where they exist, the prior kernel parent terminal and the prior acknowledgement**. It must also **name a disposition that is mechanically legal for the contradiction's recorded discovery position**. A reference that does not bind every applicable element, that is unreadable, that is not of the declared resolution-reference kind, or **whose named disposition is illegal at its recorded position**, **reconciles nothing** (FC-39, ENV-2, IDR-1).
- **What the kernel does with it — exactly two things.** It **validates** that the reference exists, is readable, is of the declared kind, binds the elements above, and names a disposition that is **mechanically legal for the recorded discovery position** — a successor generation of this same logical operation at **(A) only**, through the valid UDOK-4 path with every §H.8 prerequisite and the predecessor's committed exact-head closure (UG-10, FC-41), and at **(B)** or **(C)** **never** (§H.7, FC-27) — and it **consumes it by reference**, appending the consumption as its own coordination record (L-3f, R-19d). **Validating legality is not choosing**: the owner or the applicable policy owner selects the substantive route, and the kernel only refuses a route the position does not have. It **never chooses the disposition, never classifies the contradiction, never derives, defaults, or manufactures the reference, never infers it from silence or elapsed time, and never invents success** (AF-3, UG-4, §R.11).
- **What holds until it exists.** The contradiction stays **recovery-required**. An uncommitted UDOK-7 may not commit and an uncommitted UDOK-8 may not follow (positions A and B), and in **every** position the kernel makes **no further reliance on, reuse of, republication of, release action on, or new acknowledgement of** the contradicted completion claim (FC-38, FC-39). Committed history is untouched throughout (PC-1, PC-2, G-INV-6, UG-8).
- **What consuming it does not do.** It **does not** re-open the closed generation (MC-4, FC-36), **does not** register the child into it or backfill membership into a superseded predecessor (MC-9, MC-11, FC-36), **does not** create a successor generation of an operation whose UDOK-7 has committed (§H.7, FC-27), and **does not itself write, rewrite, recompute, or re-issue a terminal or an acknowledgement** (§H.7, §L.6, O-9, FC-40) — where **nothing had yet committed** (position A), the ordinary UDOK-7 and UDOK-8 fences simply resume under their own unchanged rules (§C.6, TB-3), and where a terminal, or a terminal and an acknowledgement, **had** committed (positions B and C), they stand as history and **no second one is ever written** (§L.7). It also **does not** decide the onward substantive treatment. **The substantive route remains the owner's and the policy's, and stays open** (MC-2, §S.4, §X) — a **successor generation of this same logical operation at position (A) only**, and at **any** position a **genuinely new logical operation** or another **authority-valid correction**. The kernel neither selects among them nor invents one; it only refuses a disposition its recorded position does not permit (FC-39).

## N. §0B LOGGING

### N.1 The law

**ONE REAL OPERATION → ONE OPERATIONAL LOG.**

### N.2 How that applies here

- The kernel coordination operation gets **one parent operational log identity** (E24).
- Its genuinely separate real stages and recovery actions may have **child operation logs** where they are genuinely separate operations under the accepted parent/child pattern — never as a device for producing extra records about the same operation.
- **Component operations retain their own logs.** The kernel never writes into them, never mirrors them, and never summarises them into its own.

### N.3 What is NOT an operational log

**Canonical state records, claims, checkpoints, receipts, snapshots, and evidence are NOT extra operational logs.** Specifically, none of the following is a §0B operational log: kernel coordination transition records (§C.7); admission records; checkpoint registrations; child registrations; cancellation/supersession request records; terminal-evidence registrations; acknowledgement records; stale/invalidation evidence records; AIC snapshots; owner claims; owner receipts.

### N.4 No double evidence

- **References do not create a second evidential vote.** A kernel record that references an owner's record adds **no** evidential weight to that owner's record, does not corroborate it, and does not make it more certain. Repetition adds no certainty.
- **No log-about-logging.** Writing a log never generates a further operation whose subject is that log.
- **No log ever becomes evidence for its own subject.**
- Duplicate-prevention events, recovery records, and retry-attempt child events **never create a second parent operational truth**.

### N.5 Design-level kernel log event requirements

At design level, the kernel's parent operational log must be able to record the following real events, with literal field names left open:

| # | Event | Must state (logically) |
|---|---|---|
| L-1 | Operation established | Operation identity; class; contract version; parent reference; initial generation |
| L-2 | **Layer-1** admission committed or refused | Which **Layer-1** prerequisite classes resolved for that generation; on refusal, which reference was missing or unreadable and the honest fail-closed reason. It never claims that any Layer-2 owner-seam prerequisite was resolved, granted, or held |

They **do not replace component effect idempotency**, and no kernel idempotency point may ever be offered as the duplicate defence for a component effect. **Cross-cutting duplicate-prevention rules.**

- **DP-1** — Duplicate prevention is **structural**, through identity, never best-effort and never by scanning for look-alikes.
- **DP-2** — A duplicate is always **recognized, skipped, and recorded** — never silently dropped and never allowed through.
- **DP-3** — A duplicate-prevention event is a canonical record, **not** a second parent operational truth (§N.4).
- **DP-4** — No kernel idempotency point may consume mutable authorization facts as key material (IDR-5).
- **DP-5** — **Attempted duplicate visible delivery is refused at the kernel fence and, independently, at B-INT-6's own fence.** The kernel's refusal never substitutes for B-INT-6's (FC-20).

## P. PARTIAL COMPLETION

### P.1 The situation

Some children completed and some did not; some effects committed and some did not; a generation was superseded mid-flight; a crash landed between two owners' commits.

### P.2 The rules

- **PC-1 — Preserve completed owner evidence.** Everything an owner durably committed stands, exactly as committed, referenced and never altered.
- **PC-2 — Never roll back or replay a committed effect merely to make the parent look atomic.** Atomicity of the coordination parent is **not** a reason to touch a component effect. The kernel has no rollback and no replay.
- **PC-3 — The parent remains incomplete until its required terminal contract is satisfied** — that is, until every member of the **current generation's** required-terminal set, as determined at the terminal-completeness fence (§M.5), has a committed UDOK-6. No aggregate, no majority, no "close enough", no timeout-based completion, and no narrowing of the set after a failure (MT-2).
- **PC-4 — Recovery resumes from durable truth**, in the lookup-first order of §F, consulting each incomplete item's owning component through that component's accepted recovery interface.
- **PC-5 — Superseded generations cannot commit remaining obsolete effects.** Work still in flight under a superseded generation fails the fence (§H) and is preserved as history; it does not get to finish "since it was nearly done".
- **PC-6 — Partial completion is never fake success.** It is reported as partial: which required terminals are registered, which are not, and which owner each outstanding one belongs to.
- **PC-7 — Partial completion is also never hidden failure.** Nothing is quietly abandoned; the outstanding items and their owners remain named in durable coordination records.
- **PC-9 — A refused post-closure registration is part of the honest picture.** A child refused because its generation was already closed remains durably recorded and visible in completion accounting as exactly that (MC-1). The parent's completeness is reported against the **closed generation's** determined required set; the refused child is reported separately, with the owner's routing reference where the owner supplies one — never merged into the parent's completion and never silently discarded.
- **PC-10 — An unresolved predecessor obligation is never closed by a generation change.** Partial completion under a predecessor generation stays partial and stays open until recovery resolves it or the owner's durable safe-supersession evidence covers it (§H.8). Moving to a successor generation does not make it complete, does not make it failed, and does not make it disappear.
- **PC-11 — Unregistered owner work is part of the honest picture, and can contradict a terminal at any position.** Where a child's owner ran without a committed UDOK-2 **for that generation** (MC-11), the parent's completeness is never reported as though that work did not exist. If the generation is still **both current and open**, membership is reconciled from owner truth before closure and the violation is recorded (MC-9, R-19). If the generation is already **closed** — or is **no longer current**, because a successor committed at UDOK-4 over its proven closed head (UG-10) — the kernel **never registers into it and never backfills it** (FC-36), and the honest report turns only on what had already committed at the parent: **(A)** with no parent terminal, the terminal and the acknowledgement **stay refused**; **(B)** with a committed terminal and no acknowledgement, the terminal is **preserved as history** with the contradiction and **prior-terminal integrity-violation evidence** recorded beside it, and the acknowledgement is **refused**; **(C)** with a committed acknowledgement, **both** stand as history with the contradiction and the **prior-terminal and prior-acknowledgement integrity-violation evidence** recorded beside them, and the prior acknowledgement is **never** reported as prevented, erased, revoked, rewritten, re-issued, or as not having happened (MC-10, R-19a … R-19c, §L.7, FC-38, FC-40). In every position the contradiction is reported as **recovery-required** until the owning component's or the applicable policy owner's typed durable resolution reference reconciles it (MC-12, FC-39), and while it stands the completion claim it contradicts is **not relied on, not reused, not republished, not released, and not re-acknowledged**. **The honest report also names which onward routes mechanically exist at that position**: a successor generation of this same logical operation at **(A) only**, through the valid UDOK-4 path with the predecessor's exact-head closure (UG-10, FC-41), and at **(B)** and **(C)** never — there, only a genuinely new logical operation or another authority-valid correction that reopens, regenerates, rewrites, revokes, and reissues nothing (§H.7, §L.7, MC-12, FC-27, FC-39). Partial completion may not be closed by ignoring work the kernel can durably see at its owner.
- **PC-8 — Per-item independence is the owner's, and is respected.** Where an accepted component establishes that a blocked item does not stop unrelated ready items (B-INT-4/B15) or that message, media, and relationship work items recover independently (B22), the kernel's parent aggregation reflects that structure and never re-couples items the owner deliberately separated.

## R. NEGATIVE PARALLEL-SYSTEM INVARIANTS

The kernel must **NEVER** become:

1. **A second retry system beside B9.** No classification, no admission, no attempt counting, no budgets, no gaps, no deadlines, no exhaustion, no retry disposition, no alternate retry classes, no second retry group.
2. **A second hold system beside B-HOLD.** No kernel hold record, no kernel release, no kernel "enough" test, no waiting around a live hold.
3. **A second ingestion claim beside B11.** No kernel ingestion claim, no root-ID reservation, no ownership generation, no append fence, no capture idempotency, no touching the sealed historical batch.
4. **A second promotion claim beside B16.** No kernel promotion claim, no evidence-sufficiency judgment, no production authorization, no second eligibility record.
5. **A second reread claim beside B10.** No kernel reread identity, no new reading layer, no conversion between retry and reread.
6. **A second AIC authority mechanism.** No authority enumeration, no authority-order proof, no establishment episode, no generation identity, no snapshot or refusal commit, no current-input re-proof, no outcome mapping, no AIC duplicate prevention, no AIC retry seam.
7. **A second privacy / access / relevance gate.** No §7Q evaluation, no SACL or PBR decision, no §7R relevance judgment, no re-ordering of privacy-before-relevance, no second door at the visible-output boundary.
8. **A generic duplicate identity overriding component canonical identities.** The kernel operation identity is never a duplicate key at any component seam.
9. **Duplicate §0B evidence.** No log-about-logging, no second parent log, no reference presented as corroboration, no canonical state record dressed up as an operational log.
10. **Generic success that bypasses component terminal evidence.** No "worker returned", no "process exited 0", no aggregate completion, no timeout-based success.
11. **An authorization / policy engine.** No policy selection, no interpretation, no defaulting, no substitution for an owner's decision.
12. **A substantive cancellation decider.** The kernel records that cancellation was requested and that an owner durably stopped; it never decides that stopping is right.

Never treat a child registration committed under one generation as membership in another, and never decide from a kernel record whether an owner may reuse a child canonical identity across generations — that is the component's own. Never erase, rewrite, recompute, revoke, re-issue, or otherwise unwind a committed kernel parent terminal or a committed acknowledgement because a contradiction was discovered afterwards, and never claim a prior acknowledgement was prevented or did not happen. Never commit a generation transition over a predecessor whose UDOK-6C membership closure has not durably committed at that predecessor's exact, unchanged current membership head, and never fold, imply, backdate, or fabricate that closure to make a transition possible. Never treat an already-committed successor lacking a valid predecessor exact-head closure as repairable rather than as a fail-closed contradiction. Never let a successor generation of the same logical operation be offered, accepted, or acted on as the disposition of a contradiction discovered after the kernel-parent terminal — with or without an acknowledgement; there, only a genuinely new logical operation or another authority-valid correction that reopens, regenerates, rewrites, revokes, and reissues nothing exists at all.
